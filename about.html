Why I do this

I moved into tech in 2021, and I kept noticing the same thing from the inside that I'd seen from the kitchen pass: companies buying tools to solve problems that were never tool problems. Technology without business context doesn't fix anything — it just relocates the mess and makes it faster. So I work the other way round: process first, then automation, then AI.

My story. Thirteen years in hospitality operations: opening a café and bakery in Donegal National Park, running four F&B outlets and 600 covers a day at the Royal Marine, rebuilding a hotel restaurant into a takeaway business in the first weeks of COVID with nothing but Facebook, Google Ads and a redrawn menu. Along the way I learned SOPs, forecasting, margin control, and what happens to a team when the process is unclear.

In 2021 I joined TTEC as a Google Ads account manager. Promoted to POD Lead in four months, then to Team Leader running Google Customer Solutions for Poland and CZ/SK, as a TTEC vendor partner. That team finished #1 for revenue globally, and I took the Platinum Club award for best manager in EMEA. I also held a 5/5 employee satisfaction score for four straight quarters — nobody left, and that protected the margin.

Then I went technical. At Sensay, an AI SaaS company, I stopped selling and started building: migrating legacy systems into HubSpot, enriching the data with Breeze Intelligence, engineering automated sequences in n8n and Zapier, and assembling Looker Studio dashboards that merged CRM, product and website analytics. Funnel engagement went up fivefold.

At SpotOn I ran client operations for a restaurant-tech platform, and the same pattern held: audit the workflows, automate the right ones, cut ticket handling time by 30%, and rebuild the SOPs so the answers live somewhere other than in people's heads. Which, given where I started, felt like the circle closing.

I'm now Associate Partner at Yameo, a Dutch–Polish software house that has been building enterprise systems for banking, insurance and healthcare since 2006. That partnership matters for one practical reason: when the work I've mapped needs engineers to build it, I have a team behind me that ships on fixed price, fixed date and a signed SLA. Most independent consultants have to hand you a plan and wish you luck.

Why work with me

You also get me — not a pitch team followed by a delivery team you've never met.
