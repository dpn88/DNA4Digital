Go-to-market and the processes underneath it

This is where I do my best work, and where most AI spending goes to die. Companies point AI at the visible layer: writing the emails, summarising the calls, generating the content. Underneath, the pipeline stages mean different things to different people, leads sit in a gap between marketing and sales for four days, the CRM has three fields for the same thing, and nobody has written down what "qualified" means.

Automating that doesn't fix it. It scales it. So I work both layers, in order: make the revenue process legible, then apply AI where it compounds.

Revenue process mapping

The full journey from first touch to closed and renewed. Stage definitions, exit criteria, owners, and every handoff where things currently fall over.

Funnel & pipeline hygiene

Qualification rules that hold, forecast inputs you can trust, CRM fields people actually fill in because they're worth filling in.

GTM automation

Research, enrichment, routing, follow-up, call notes and reporting. Applied to a process that works, so the gains stick.

Enablement for revenue teams

SDRs, AEs, marketing and CS trained on the workflows for their role, using your data and your deals. Not a generic prompt library.

What you get: a mapped GTM process, a list of the friction points ranked by cost, and a 90-day plan splitting them into "fix the process" and "automate it."
